Right, so we talked about this gauss law ($\vec{\nabla} \dot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$), but in what type of situations can we apply. What does it tell us, and how can we use it?

Well, in principle lets recap what it means. The divergence of an electric field is going to be equal to the charge density inside. Where the divergence measures how much is flowing in / out of a point.
This means that if we have no charges inside, the divergence will be 0, which means that all the fields that come in have to come out. As an example, this statement tells us that we are not able to balance a test charge
inside our field. Even if we surrounded a test charge by positive charges, the Guass Law does not permit the test charge to remain balanced and stationary. For that effect to happen, there has to be a non-electric
external force acting upon the test charge, like the walls of a glass tube.
